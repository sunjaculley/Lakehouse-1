Meeting Recap
Summary
Microsite Purpose And Audience
Muccioli and McMahon explained that the team is creating a highly specific, invitation-only microsite for KPMGT’s 100 Lake Nona Impact Forum delegates staying at Lakehouse, while coordinating with the broader event experience and stakeholder group.
Event Context
KPMGT is a longstanding co-branding partner and premier sponsor of the annual Lake Nona Impact Forum, which covers health and technology topics and is held primarily at Lakehouse for two of its three days, with the third day at GuideWell. The event is invitation-only, cannot be purchased through public ticketing, and typically includes approximately 700 attendees.
Target Audience
The microsite is intended for KPMGT’s 100 sponsor delegate badges and their associated Lakehouse experience, not for all Lake Nona Impact Forum attendees. The delegates include clients allocated through multiple KPMGT lines of business, as well as KPMGT personnel supporting those client relationships.
Experience Strategy
Muccioli described the microsite as providing a highly personalized, premium experience for invited delegates. Its purpose is to help deepen client relationships by showing how KPMGT is engaged in health and technology while avoiding an overtly promotional or thought-leadership-heavy presentation.
Brand Hierarchy
The historical hierarchy has placed the Lake Nona Impact Forum first, KPMGT second, and Lakehouse third. For this year’s experience, KPMGT and the event will receive greater emphasis, with Lakehouse positioned as an elevated setting and differentiator that gives guests convenient access to the conference.
Microsite Content And Design
The project team agreed to adapt the existing client ‘Know Before You Go’ microsite structure into a more event-specific experience using shared copy, visuals, schedules, arrival information, FAQs, maps, and speaker content.
Template Adaptation
The new microsite should closely reuse the existing Medtronic Tax Vision/client microsite template rather than remain evergreen or generic. It will be labeled for the 2026 Lake Nona Impact Forum, include KPMGT and Lakehouse branding, and display the relevant event dates and locations.
Event Information
The site should include the event agenda as it becomes available, day-by-day information for arrival and activities, speaker names and biographies when approved, and information covering the Lakehouse portion of the event and the third day at GuideWell. ‘Coming soon’ placeholders may be used when content is not yet cleared for publication.
Arrival And Travel
The site should retain transportation guidance from the client microsite, including driving, Brightline, and flight options, while adding Lakehouse-specific arrival instructions. McMahon emphasized that the page should centralize travel surveys, shuttle directions, accommodation review links, ambassador details, and other logistics to reduce repeated email exchanges. 
FAQs And Visuals
The team will reuse the newly designed FAQ structure, organizing information into clear categories so users can find details without being overwhelmed. The microsite should use high-quality assets from the new photo shoot, the existing drone footage for the header, and a 3D Lakehouse map with selectable areas such as food and beverage and entertainment.
Mobile App Decision
The team decided not to direct guests to a separate Lakehouse app. McMahon explained that the official Cvent app will provide the Lake Nona Impact Forum agenda and push notifications, while Lakehouse information will be handled through the microsite and other event communications.
Client-Specific Gated Experiences
Monahan, McMahon, Muccioli, and Culley designed a shared microsite with password-protected pages for approximately five priority sponsors, allowing each client to receive customized activities, branding, contacts, and logistics without maintaining separate site instances.
Priority Sponsors
The five priority sponsor groups identified were Verizon, Oracle, Alphabet, Johnson & Johnson, and Microsoft. These organizations independently sponsor the Lake Nona Impact Forum and may receive additional KPMGT-hosted Lakehouse experiences, such as private sessions, networking, dinners, or recreational activities before the main conference.
Personalized Pages
Each client-specific page should visibly identify the organization through its logo and welcome copy, then provide the relevant client lead partner, KPMGT ambassador, contact information, and customized event details. The page content may include dates, times, locations, registration instructions, and activities unique to that client.
Shared Site Structure
The preferred structure is one universal landing page containing common event information and links or buttons for the priority sponsors, followed by individual gated pages. This avoids creating five or more separate microsite instances and allows shared agenda, speaker, FAQ, and logistics content to be updated centrally.
Delegate Distribution
McMahon clarified that the 100 KPMGT delegate badges are distributed across several lines of business, including healthcare, consumer and retail, technology, and life sciences. The five priority sponsors represent a distinct subset receiving additional attention; not every one of the 100 delegates will necessarily belong to those five companies, and supporting KPMGT personnel may also receive access to the relevant client page.
Access Control
The site should not be indexed or searchable. Muccioli proposed distributing a common URL while manually providing the appropriate password for each client-specific section, so users can see the shared page but access only the page associated with their assigned organization.
Gating Experience
Culley described the likely technical pattern as a universal landing page, a client button, a password gate, and then the branded client page. Muccioli asked the team to explore a more streamlined interaction in which clicking the client option leads directly to a password prompt before revealing the destination page; Culley agreed to investigate whether that approach is feasible.
Stakeholder Coordination And Delivery
Muccioli and Milmoe assigned follow-up work to produce an initial wireframe, compile approved content, update the project timeline, and review the first draft with the broader LNIF stakeholder team with an intended completion by the end of November.
Wireframe Review

The first wireframe does not need to be fully polished; it should show all discussed content elements, the universal landing page, and the proposed password-protected experience so Muccioli and McMahon can review it with the broader LNIF stakeholder group.
Stakeholder Feedback
Muccioli and McMahon will bring the first wireframe to their recurring weekly LNIF stakeholder call, collect feedback, and relay the resulting changes to the project team.
Content Inputs
Muccioli and McMahon will organize the FAQ and logistics information into the same categories used for the existing client microsite. They will also provide approved speaker information and other content that Gloria and the relevant stakeholders have cleared for publication.
Reference Materials
Milmoe will send McMahon the existing client microsite and the finalized FAQ content prepared with Courtney. Muccioli will also provide the Lake Nona Impact Forum website link so the team can accurately combine event overview copy with links back to the official event site.
Target Timing
Milmoe will confirm the creative team’s copy and production availability and send a schedule for the first draft. The working target is to complete the microsite by the end of November, with the team acknowledging that the turnaround is short because of the upcoming Thanksgiving period.
